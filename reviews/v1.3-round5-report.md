# v1.3 round 5

Same branch and the same pull request. Test seeds 4001–4030 were not used. Tune is 101–110. Dev is 1–10. The run is `r6/results/v13_round5`.

X is not “S first, then X”. Round 4’s design, a finished same-truck loop followed by cross moves on the evaluations it left over, is reverted. There is no post-search cross tail, and there is no rule that a plan may be published only when a cross sortie remains. From ALNS iteration 1, every iteration of X’s nominal search evaluates one cross move beside the same-truck repair. A cross that improves v1.3 J becomes the champion immediately. Later iterations keep proposing crosses, including from that champion. The same-truck incumbent is what the next slice continues from, so a cross does not erase it, and the published plan is whichever of the two has the better v1.3 J.

## Hard constraints

- [x] **No 0.2 failure weight, for S or for X.** Unchanged. Planning uses `3/(2N)` and `1/(N(N−1))`. `tests/test_v13_acceptance.py` is 14 passed, including the test that fails if a v1.3 planner reads 0.2.
- [x] **X is one integrated search.** Cross moves run inside the nominal loop from iteration 1. They are not a second phase and they do not wait for the same-truck search to finish. Recovery remains one catalog.
- [x] **No experiment setting was changed.** Rules, failure model, probabilities, budgets (1000/1000, 60 s), seeds, fleet, energy, H, prices, statistics, and the 0.08333 kWh margin are unchanged. S’s operators are unchanged. One schedule fix is shared with S and is described below.

## Schedule fix, applied to S and X

Annealing used to cool with the evaluation counter. A cross evaluation advanced that counter, so it changed X’s temperature and could end a 150-evaluation slice early. The nominal schedule is now the iteration count. Each of the two 150-evaluation slices is 30 iterations (`nominal_start_slice // 5`), and the long phase cools over the next 120 iterations. The same function runs for S and for X. A cross evaluation no longer changes the temperature or the slice length. The 1000-evaluation cap still stops the search.

## What X’s search does

Inside every nominal iteration, after the same-truck repair, X evaluates one cross move. The move rotates among three constructions: reassigning up to three same-truck recoveries onto other trucks at once, handing a ground customer on the longest truck to a drone that meets a shorter truck, and the single best recovery retarget. The failure term inside J, for X only, may use a cheaper cross-rescue proxy when another truck already visits a customer the drone can reach. The weights stay `3/(2N)` and `1/(N(N−1))`. S does not use that proxy. The 0.08333 kWh margin still rejects an illegal cross. Recovery still offers cross rescues in the one catalog; this round only counts how often one is chosen.

## What was not changed

The 1000/1000 budgets, the 60 s wall, the 0.08333 kWh margin, H, the failure model, and the prices stay as specified. A larger cross search, or a margin that let the cheaper illegal crosses through, might move the numbers. Those are setting changes, and they were not made.
